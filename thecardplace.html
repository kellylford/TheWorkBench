The Card Place

Card games built to be played by anyone — with a mouse, with a keyboard alone, or entirely by ear with a screen reader. No sign up, no download, nothing to install.

Choose a game

  • Sheephead

    3 to 6 players · you against the computer

    The Wisconsin classic, also called Schafkopf. Queens and jacks are trump, the picker takes the blind, and the jack of diamonds is a secret partner nobody admits to.

    • Every card is a button that says what it is and whether you can play it
    • Review keys for your hand, the trick, the scores and the play order
    • Choose the pace, including one play at a time
    Play Sheephead

  • Euchre

    4 players · on your own, or with other people

    The Midwest and Ontario standard. Two partnerships, five tricks a hand, first to ten points — and the jack of the same colour as trump quietly becomes a trump too.

    • Both bowers are named as bowers wherever a card is read out
    • While you bid, it tells you what your hand would be worth with that suit as trump
    • Play with friends using a five-letter code; empty seats are played by the computer
    Play Euchre

  • Cribbage

    2 players · you against the computer

    Pegging, fifteens, runs and the crib, scored to 121 on a board you can read as well as see. The classic two-hander.

    • Keyboard playable, with the board exposed as a live score
    • A running game log you can read back at your own pace
    • Full rules included for anyone learning

Why these exist

Most card games online are unplayable without sight. These are built the other way round: the game state is text first, and the pictures are added on top where they cannot get in the way.

  • Nothing is conveyed by sight alone. Every card, score and turn is announced, and every visual flourish is hidden from assistive technology so it is never read out twice.
  • Your screen reader stays in charge. Nothing is marked as an application region, so browse, focus and forms modes all behave normally.
  • Everything scales. Turn your text size up and the layout follows, including the cards.
  • Keyboard only is a first-class way to play, not a fallback.

Sheephead and Cribbage run entirely in your browser and send nothing anywhere. Euchre does too until you choose to open a table with other people — from then on it sends your moves and your chosen name to the room, and nothing else. There are no accounts, and a table is a five-letter code that stops existing when everybody leaves.
