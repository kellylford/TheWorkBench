Hearts

Four players, thirteen tricks, no trump — and the lowest score wins.

Play with other people

Anyone can join a table with its code. There are no accounts and nothing to install. A hearts table is always four seats, and any seat nobody is sitting in is played by the computer — so a table works with two people, or three, or four.

Five letters and numbers. Upper or lower case both work, and spaces and dashes are ignored. The letters O, I and L and the digits zero and one are never used, so there is nothing to mistake for anything else.

Your table code

Send the code to the people you want to play with, wait for them to appear in the seats below, then start. Any seat still empty is played by the computer, so you can start whenever you like.

      How hearts works

      The object

      Take as few points as possible. Every heart is one point and the queen of spades is thirteen — twenty-six in every hand. When somebody reaches the target the game ends, and the player with the lowest score wins.

      Passing

      Before each hand you give three cards away and are given three in return. Everybody chooses at the same time, so you commit before you see what is coming. The direction rotates: left, right, across, and then a hand where nobody passes at all.

      The play

      • Whoever holds the two of clubs leads it. That is not a choice.
      • Follow the suit led if you can. There is no trump, so the highest card of the suit led takes the trick.
      • No hearts and no queen of spades on the first trick, unless that is all you hold.
      • Hearts cannot be led until somebody has discarded one on another suit. That is what “hearts are broken” means.

      Shooting the moon

      Take all twenty-six points and the hand turns inside out: you score nothing and everybody else scores twenty-six. It is worth watching for — if somebody has taken every heart so far, letting them have one is cheaper than letting them have the rest.

      Playing by keyboard

      Every shortcut below is also a button on screen, so nothing is only reachable one way.

      • Left and Right move along your hand; Enter plays or selects the card you are on.
      • H reads your hand.
      • T reads the trick so far.
      • S reads the scores.
      • P reads what points have been taken this hand.
      • W says who is at the table.
      • Enter on a button does what the button says.
